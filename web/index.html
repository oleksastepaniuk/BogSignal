Europe’s Amazon

Across a roughly 186,000 km² region, Polissia is a mosaic of rivers, floodplains, peatlands and forests — one of Europe’s great wetland landscapes. These habitats store carbon, regulate water and provide vital refuge for wildlife and migratory birds.

Decades of drainage, agricultural expansion and infrastructure development have put Polissia's wetlands under pressure. But efforts to reverse this damage are underway. Conservation initiatives such as Wild Polissia are working to bring water back to drained wetlands, aiming to restore up to 20,000 hectares in Ukraine alone.

Seeing beyond the surface

To protect wetlands, we first need to understand where they are — and be able to follow how they change. Satellites can cover enormous areas repeatedly, but clouds and vegetation can conceal important clues.

We tested whether NISAR could add useful information to existing satellite observations, as a first step toward a stronger baseline for future monitoring.

Sentinel-2

What the landscape looks like

Reflected sunlight reveals vegetation, open water and land cover. Clouds block the view, and a canopy can hide water below.

Sentinel-1 · C-band

A different perspective

Radar works day and night through clouds. Backscatter responds to surface roughness, moisture and vegetation structure.

NISAR · L-band

Looking deeper into vegetation

NISAR’s longer-wavelength radar can penetrate further into some vegetation canopies, providing additional clues about woody structure and water beneath vegetation. These signals alone do not confirm a bog.

02 — Knowing where to look first

The highest-scoring 5% of our validation area contained 73.4% of the swamp and marsh pixels in our reference maps. This suggests BogSignal could help researchers narrow their search, prioritizing promising areas for closer investigation.

A first step toward better-informed conservation

BogSignal can help focus wetland research on places worth a closer look. The next steps are independent checks of promising locations and repeated observations to investigate change. Identifying wetland signals does not establish restoration suitability.

Full results & methods

We used U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder initialized from ImageNet weights. The encoder extracts image features, while the decoder combines them with finer spatial detail to predict a class for each pixel. The same architecture and training recipe were used for each sensor combination, with input channels adapted to the selected sensors and their validity masks. The model predicts six classes; for the displayed wetland results, Swamp and Marsh probabilities are added before choosing among five combined classes.

Detailed experiment results

A-D share 585 validation patches and 37,411,425 scored pixels. Wetland means Swamp + Marsh; water and peatland classes remain separate.

IoU measures overlap. Recall measures reference wetlands recovered; reference precision measures agreement among predicted wetlands. The temporal follow-up has a smaller matched footprint: compare TD only with the matched D values in its row, not directly with A-D above. TD averages NISAR linear power over 3-5 observations before dB conversion; processing, dates and training populations also differ. It improved reference precision but reduced recall, so D remains highlighted.

Our experiment uses radar backscatter power (GCOV for NISAR), not interferometric phase. Bright radar returns alone do not establish wetland type or peat presence. The sensor comparison asks whether adding NISAR improves agreement with weak reference labels.

Inputs: Sentinel-2 optical bands, Sentinel-1 C-band backscatter and NISAR L-band GCOV power. A six-class U-Net uses a ResNet-34 encoder; displayed maps sum Swamp and Marsh probabilities before five-class selection.

  • Single seed (17), validation against historical/inferred weak references. Labels can be incomplete, wrong or temporally mismatched; Other is not verified dry land.
  • Validation informed checkpoint selection. No test-set results or independent field validation are available.
  • Review budget means scored area, not a count of sites. Scores are uncalibrated and do not confirm wetlands.
  • These experiments do not establish restoration suitability or verified discovery of new wetland sites.
