Revealing Europe's Hidden Wetlands

We cannot protect what we cannot see. BogSignal explores how NISAR can help us identify Polissia's wetlands — and focus conservation research where it is needed.

Landscape Why radar Experiment Findings Sources

Europe’s Amazon

Across a roughly 186,000 km² region, Polissia is a vast mosaic of rivers, floodplains, peatlands and forests spanning Ukraine, Belarus, Poland and Russia. These wetlands store carbon, regulate water and provide vital habitats for wildlife and migratory birds.

Decades of drainage, agriculture and infrastructure development have transformed parts of this landscape. But efforts to reverse the damage are underway. In Ukraine, the Wild Polissia initiative aims to restore up to 20,000 hectares of wetlands. Restoration work at Somyne and plans for Syra Pohonia illustrate how conservationists are working to bring water back to drained peatlands. Yet restoring wetlands raises a bigger question: How can we understand where wetlands remain, where they have changed, and which areas deserve closer investigation? Polissia is enormous, and some wetlands are hidden beneath forest canopies. Field surveys alone cannot cover the entire region. That’s where BogSignal comes in. We explore whether NISAR’s new radar observations can help researchers identify potential wetlands and establish a baseline for monitoring future change.

Seeing beyond the surface

To protect wetlands, we need to understand where they are and how they change. Satellites offer repeated observations across vast landscapes, but clouds and dense vegetation can conceal important clues.

From the Amazon to Polissia. Earlier studies in the Amazon demonstrated how L-band radar can map flooded vegetation, including water concealed beneath forest canopies. In some conditions, radar signals reflect between standing water and tree trunks, helping reveal otherwise hidden inundation.

Inspired by this research, BogSignal asks: Can NISAR's L-band radar improve wetland mapping beyond what Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 already provide?

Sentinel-2

What the landscape looks like

Reflected sunlight reveals vegetation, open water and land cover. Clouds block the view, and a canopy can hide water below.

Sentinel-1 · C-band

A different perspective

Radar works day and night through clouds. Backscatter responds to surface roughness, moisture and vegetation structure.

NISAR · L-band Study focus

Looking deeper into vegetation

NISAR’s longer-wavelength radar can penetrate further into some vegetation canopies, providing additional clues about woody structure and water beneath vegetation. These signals alone do not confirm a bog.

Putting NISAR to the test

We trained the same AI segmentation model with two sets of satellite inputs: without NISAR (Sentinel-2 optical imagery + Sentinel-1 radar) and with NISAR (the same inputs plus NISAR).

Our approach combines two radar wavelengths (C-band and L-band), four polarization channels (VV, VH, HH and HV), and observations from both ascending and descending satellite passes. Together, they provide complementary information about vegetation, moisture and surface structure.

We compared both models against the same historical and inferred wetland references over the same validation area, allowing us to measure what NISAR adds.

Understanding the radar images

NISAR images use illustrative false colors: red represents HH polarization, green represents HV, and blue represents their contrast. Both ascending and descending observations are included in the model, while the displayed NISAR imagery uses ascending observations. Radar colors represent differences in the returned signal, not confirmed wetland types.

Exploratory comparison: NISAR observations are provisional. Differences may reflect seasonal conditions, acquisition geometry or data quality. Results have not been independently validated and do not establish restoration impacts. White areas indicate missing imagery.

Results & methods

Data & model

Satellite inputs
Sentinel-2 optical imagery, Sentinel-1 C-band radar (VV/VH) and NISAR L-band radar (HH/HV), using ascending and descending observations.
AI model
A U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder predicts six land-cover classes: Swamp, Marsh, Water, Peatland cropland, Peatland non-cropland, and Other.
Experiment
Four versions of the same model compare different satellite combinations to measure what NISAR adds beyond existing observations.
Radar measurements
We analyze radar backscatter, not interferometric phase. Swamp and Marsh are combined for the headline wetland results.

Limitations

Historical references
Our labels come from historical and inferred wetland maps, which may be incomplete, inaccurate or outdated.
Preliminary evaluation
Results come from one training run, with validation used for model selection. No independent test-set or field evaluation is available.
Unverified predictions
Model scores help rank areas for investigation, but do not confirm wetland presence, degradation or restoration suitability.
Selected examples
The interactive examples illustrate successes and failures rather than representing average model performance.

Detailed experiment results

A-D share 585 validation patches and 37,411,425 scored pixels. Wetland means Swamp + Marsh; water and peatland classes remain separate.

IoU measures overlap. Recall measures reference wetlands recovered; reference precision measures agreement among predicted wetlands. The temporal follow-up has a smaller matched footprint: compare TD only with the matched D values in its row, not directly with A-D above. TD averages NISAR linear power over 3-5 observations before dB conversion; processing, dates and training populations also differ. It improved reference precision but reduced recall, so D remains highlighted.

NASA & Partner Resources Behind BogSignal

We drew on NASA’s open-data ecosystem and educational resources throughout BogSignal’s development — from acquiring NISAR observations to interpreting radar measurements and designing our interactive presentation.

Data discovery & access

  • NASA Common Metadata Repository (CMR) APIPowered our automated NISAR data-discovery pipeline, allowing us to identify available acquisitions, retrieve observation dates and map satellite coverage across Polissia.
  • NASA Earthdata SearchHelped us explore NASA Earth-observation collections and understand the available satellite products.
  • ASF VertexProvided a visual reference for exploring SAR data availability and product options. We acquired NISAR science products through ASF using Earthdata authentication.

Technical development

  • NISAR Data User GuideOur primary technical reference for understanding NISAR products, radar polarizations, GCOV backscatter measurements and data-access workflows.
  • CSA SAR Python/QGIS TutorialA practical reference we consulted for working with SAR imagery in Python and QGIS.

Scientific foundations

  • Hess et al. (2003) — Dual-season mapping of wetland inundation and vegetation for the central Amazon basinResearch on L-band radar mapping of inundation and vegetation in the Amazon, motivating our interest in wetlands beneath forest canopies.
  • Hübinger et al. (2026) — Exploring the potential of using L-band InSAR for mapping flooded vegetation in tropical wetlandsAn ALOS PALSAR-1 InSAR study in Colombia's Atrato floodplain; its phase-based approach differs from our backscatter experiment.
  • Hübinger & Jaramillo (2026) — Hydrological barriers compound pressure on the world's most threatened wetlandsA preprint investigating hydrological barriers using radar interferometry, including NISAR GSLC observations.
  • NISAR Applications White PapersHelped us connect radar observations to real-world applications, particularly flood mapping, water resources and environmental monitoring.
  • NASA Introduction to Remote SensingProvided background for understanding satellite measurements, spectral information and spatial resolution.
  • NASA Introduction to SARHelped us explain radar wavelengths, polarizations and scattering mechanisms — essential concepts behind our comparison of Sentinel-1 and NISAR.
  • NASA ARSETProvided access to applied remote-sensing training materials and guidance on SAR and NISAR applications.

Visualization & storytelling

  • NISAR StoryMapsServed as references for communicating complex radar observations through accessible visual stories and interactive maps.
  • UAVSAR PostcardsProvided educational examples of radar imagery and how different surface features can be represented visually.
